What This Video Covers

This podcast episode features Dr. Karny Ilan, co-founder and CEO of Feminai, discussing an app-connected disposable breast patch designed to support at-home breast monitoring with physician review. The conversation covers clinical trials, engineering, risk questionnaires, dense breasts, and access gaps in breast screening.

Key takeaway: The episode frames AI and at-home monitoring as a way to support access and change tracking, not as a replacement for clinical screening or physician decision-making.

What the At-Home Tool Is Designed to Do

Dr. Ilan describes a disposable wearable breast patch that connects through an app. The app uses a medical questionnaire to help stratify risk and guide how often a person should perform the exam.

The scan takes a few minutes, the patch is discarded, and the physician receives results to decide whether further examination is needed. The goal is a smoother bridge between home monitoring and clinical follow-up.

Why Physician-Led Design Matters

The transcript repeatedly returns to the value of combining medical and engineering perspectives. Dr. Ilan is a physician, while her co-founders bring data science and biomedical engineering expertise.

That multidisciplinary design matters because the product has to fit different bodies, handle medical data securely, and be validated through regulated clinical pathways. The team discusses successful clinical trials in Israel and the United States.

Dense Breasts, Change Over Time, and Access

Sheree and Dr. Ilan discuss dense breast tissue, different breast sizes and textures, and the difficulty of relying only on touch. They also discuss comparing results over time, so a change can be interpreted against a person previous pattern.

The episode is especially relevant for people who face access barriers, delayed appointments, or uncertainty about how often they should be checked. It still emphasizes clinical oversight.

What to Watch For

Transcript-informed checkpoints

  • App plus disposable wearable breast patch
  • Risk grouping through a medical questionnaire
  • Scan results routed to a physician
  • Designed for different breast shapes, sizes, textures, and densities
  • Clinical trials and regulation are central to responsible use

When to Get Help

If you notice a new breast change, contact a healthcare provider. At-home tools and self-exams should not delay diagnostic imaging or clinical evaluation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this replace mammograms?

No. The episode presents at-home AI monitoring as a support tool connected to physician review, not a replacement for guideline-based imaging.

How does the patch work?

Dr. Ilan describes an app-connected disposable breast patch that performs a scan in a few minutes and sends results for physician review.

Why are dense breasts discussed?

Dense tissue can make breast monitoring more complex, and the product is being designed with different breast sizes, shapes, textures, and densities in mind.

What role do clinical trials play?

The founders discuss clinical trials and regulation as essential steps for proving the technology is safe, useful, and medically responsible.
